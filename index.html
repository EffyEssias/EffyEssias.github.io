About me

Hello! I'm Fernando, but online I'm Effy. I'm a Brazilian UI/UX designer and Roblox developer. I started making games the moment I joined Roblox in 2014, started designing UI in 2022, and I've been improving my skills ever since.

I started drawing at 4, and I love to brainstorm and sketch ideas daily. I can use Figma or draw in my favourite drawing software, then I script it all in Luau inside Roblox Studio.

To me, making UI is one of the most important things in a game. It's what connects the game to the player. Through it, players can change themselves virtually, buy digital products, interact with each other and much more.

  • Phantom Inc.

    Own project, 2025

    • Unreleased
    • Procreate
    • Aseprite
    • Luau

    Why it looks this way

    • The HUD stays in the corners so the middle of the screen is clear. In a horror game you need to see what is coming.
    • Health, stamina and the bunny portrait are grouped bottom-left, so your status is one glance away.
    • Neon colours stay readable in the dark rooms.

    A horror game inspired by Dandy's World, Luigi's Mansion and Animal Crossing, where you play as an animal investigator who must survive cryptic animal monsters while uncovering the truth about the Animal Forest. I drew several HUD concepts until one felt right, then drew the detailed icons like the gun and flashlight in Procreate and the bunny pixel sticker in Aseprite, and built the simpler ones, like the 1 and 2 slots, in Studio. It's a complex passion project, so I've paused it until I can fund a team to help me develop it.

  • My Avatar Catalog

    Solo study project, 2025

    • Study project
    • Avatar editor
    • Pixel art
    • Luau

    What I studied

    • Why the live preview sits on the left: players see each item on their avatar right away.
    • How search and category tabs keep thousands of items easy to browse.
    • Why a Try button comes before Buy: players test an item first, so they don’t buy something they regret.
    • My own part: the minimal pixel art theme, with dark cards so the item pictures stand out.

    An avatar catalog with a minimal pixel art theme, made to study and improve my UI and scripting skills. Its layout is inspired by Catalog Avatar Creator. It has a searchable catalog with category tabs and a live avatar preview, outfit tools to adjust, buy, save and reset, plus emotes and a points system.

  • My Market

    Solo, self-owned game, 2023

    • Unreleased
    • Figma
    • Luau

    Why it looks this way

    • The Store suggests without pushing: the best value bundle gets a crown and its own bright card, and discounts get a clear -50% tag, so good deals are easy to spot.
    • Every price sits on the same green button with the Robux icon, so players always know what they are paying.
    • Eggs show the chance of each pet, so players know what they can get before they buy.
    • Tabs split limited items, game passes, money and potions, so players find what they want fast.
    • Locked markets show in Teleport, so players see what they are working toward.
    • The prize wheel shows the odds and a free spin timer, so it is clear and gives a reason to come back.

    A game about expanding a cute market, where you farm, do chores and complete quests to earn money. It's the project where Figma became part of my real workflow. I picked the art style, designed every menu in Figma, then imported and scripted them in Roblox, testing until they worked and looked right on phone, tablet and PC.

  • Bake a Squishy Dumpling

    Solo, self-owned game, 2026

    • Trend test
    • Nano Banana Pro
    • Procreate
    • Luau

    Why it looks this way

    • The shop is designed as a shopping basket and the ingredient shop as a market stall, so both feel part of the world.
    • A free option next to the paid one, a video or Robux, plus an auto buy box, so players are never stuck.

    A quick game I made solo in under a month for the Squishy Dumpling trend, to test Roblox simulator trends and AI art. On my other projects I only use AI to explore ideas from my own sketches and draw the final UI myself, but remaking every piece by hand would have taken too long for a trend test. I also use AI such as Claude to speed up my progress and assist me with big systems and complex bugs, so I can ship faster while keeping quality.

Design process

How I take a UI from a paper sketch to a working game.

  1. Sketch

    I brainstorm the game's UI with quick sketches on paper.

  2. Explore styles

    I redraw the idea digitally in several styles to find the best one.

  3. Improve with AI

    I use ChatGPT for improvements and ideas I hadn't thought of.

  4. Choose

    I pick the direction that matches the game best.

  5. Final art

    I rebuild it in Figma. If it needs drawing, I paint it in Procreate or Clip Studio.

  6. Import

    I export everything to Roblox and organize it in Studio.

  7. Script

    I script the UI in Luau so it works in the game.

  8. Test

    I check it on different devices and catch bugs fast, with Claude's help.

  9. Feedback

    I collect feedback and keep improving it.
