Brookhaven UI redesign

A new look for Brookhaven's welcome screen and shop. I kept the game's own layout, so players have nothing to relearn, and redrew every piece of the UI in my own style.

  • My roleUI design and illustration
  • ToolsFigma, Procreate
  • TypeConcept, made on my own
welcome_screen_style_a

A concept project. I am not affiliated with Voldex or Brookhaven, and I had no access to their data.

Research

I played Brookhaven and studied its welcome screen and shop. Both work well: the layout is simple, the text is easy to read, and players reach Play or an item in one or two taps. The look is clean and simple: a see-through updates panel, and a white shop with black text. I wanted to explore how the same screens could carry more of the town's personality, without moving anything.

Player flow

I mapped the two journeys these screens belong to and counted the taps. Both are already short: one tap from the welcome screen to playing, and three taps from playing to the purchase prompt. That is why I left the flows alone. A visual update should never add a step.

Layout

This is Brookhaven's own layout. I studied why it works before redrawing it, and changed nothing about where things sit. Players already know where everything is, and a studio could swap in the new art without rebuilding the screens. The phone versions are my own proposal: bigger buttons, a list that scrolls and nothing important past the safe area.

Welcome screen

  1. The logo sits at the top, so it's the first thing players read.
  2. Play is the biggest button, low and centred where thumbs reach.
  3. Updates sit on the side: there for players who want them, never blocking Play.
  4. The middle is left empty, so players see their avatar and the town.

Shop

  1. Tabs on the left keep every category one tap away.
  2. The title bar always shows which tab is open.
  3. The close button sits in the corner, away from the buy buttons.
  4. The biggest offers get the biggest cards.
  5. Every price is on the same green button, so players always know what they pay.

Thumb reach on a phone

On a phone held sideways, thumbs rest in the bottom corners. The green areas are the easiest to reach. The shop tabs and the lower price buttons sit inside them. Play is centred at the bottom, a short reach for either thumb, and nothing important passes the red safe-area lines.

Final UI

These are three separate style studies on Brookhaven's layout: two for the welcome screen and a third for the shop. I made them different on purpose, to show range. In a real update I would pick one style and apply it to every screen.

On the welcome screen, style A is glossy and bright, inspired by Brookhaven itself: its town signs, shop fronts and red and green colours. The welcome sign looks like a town sign, and the updates panel became a market stall with a striped awning and a label for each group. Style B is calm and hand-drawn, inspired by Animal Crossing: a hanging wooden sign with vines, a leaf-shaped Play button and the updates on a paper scroll.

The shop has its own, more cartoony style. Its buttons are close to style A's, glossy and chunky so they look pressable and are easy to tap for young players, but the panels are soft blue with a bright title bar instead of the town signs. The offers, prices and gift buttons stay where players expect them.

Which intro style I chose

Style A is my pick. It is inspired by Brookhaven itself, with its town signs, shop fronts and red and green colours, so the game still looks like itself, and its bold shapes read fast on a small screen. Style B is a twist: a calm, nature look inspired by Animal Crossing, to show how far the same layout can go in another direction.

Result

Three style studies on two screens, all on Brookhaven's own layout. Next, I would build them in Roblox Studio and test them with young players on phones: do they find Play and the price buttons as fast as today, and which style do they like best?
